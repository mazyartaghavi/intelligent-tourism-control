# An Empirical User Study on Congestion-Aware Route Recommendation

**Citation:** Kun Yi, Xisha Jin, Zhengyang Bai, Yuntao Kong, and Qiang Ma (2024). *Information and Communication Technologies in Tourism 2024*, pp. 325-338. Published conference chapter. [Publisher full text and DOI](https://link.springer.com/chapter/10.1007/978-3-031-58839-6_35).

**Reviewed:** 2026-10-17. Full-text Sections 3-6: methodology, respondents, comparisons, and regression analysis.

## Findings

The study collects evaluations from 41 respondents for five routing methods across five scenarios. Respondents assess scheduling, visiting order, distance, comfort, and overall experience. The comparisons show that stronger congestion or diversification considerations do not necessarily yield better-rated routes.

## Relevance to This Repository

Our interpretation: congestion-aware recommendation should balance distribution objectives with travel time and individual preferences. This informs the recommendation utility in the [mathematical framework](../../methodology/mathematical-framework.md#5-congestion-aware-recommendation).

## Limitations

A small, single-destination questionnaire study does not establish population-wide preferences or demonstrate actual congestion reduction after deployment. Ratings of presented routes differ from observed travel behavior.

## Proposed Next Step

Compare preference-only and congestion-aware rankings using both route-quality measures and simulated occupancy; later assess acceptance through an appropriately designed user study.
