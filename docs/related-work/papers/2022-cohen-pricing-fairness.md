# Price Discrimination with Fairness Constraints

**Citation** — Maxime C. Cohen, Adam N. Elmachtoub, and Xiao Lei (2022). *Management Science*, 68(12), 8536–8552. Published journal article. [DOI 10.1287/mnsc.2022.4317](https://pubsonline.informs.org/doi/10.1287/mnsc.2022.4317).

**Reviewed 2026-11-26.** [Author-hosted published full text](https://maxccohen.github.io/Pricing-Fairness.pdf), Sections 2.2–2.3, 3.1, and 5.1, including Theorem 1 and Proposition 1.

## Findings

The study analyzes four fairness criteria for pricing. Theorem 1 rules out exact simultaneous satisfaction of all four when two groups' mean valuations differ. Under the stated linear demand model, a modest price-fairness requirement can improve total welfare, while further tightening can reduce welfare relative to the unconstrained case. The analysis shows that welfare and access outcomes depend on the demand model and choice of criterion.

## Relevance to This Repository

Our interpretation is that the pricing evaluation should retain a common public ticket price for each service and time slot and assess how affordability and access vary across visitor groups, alongside revenue. Equal posted prices alone do not guarantee equal access. [Pricing framework](../../methodology/mathematical-framework.md#4-dynamic-pricing-as-sequential-decision-making)

## Limitations

The analytical model assumes known valuation distributions and a profit-maximizing seller. It does not model tourism's shared capacity, queues, or repeated decisions. The results do not quantify welfare under real visitor demand.

## Proposed Next Step

Compare uniform fixed and common time-slot prices in a synthetic simulator with capacity and an overall price cap. Audit group booking rates, consumer surplus, rejected requests, and revenue using predeclared synthetic valuation distributions. This experiment is proposed.
