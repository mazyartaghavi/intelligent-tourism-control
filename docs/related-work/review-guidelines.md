# Literature Review Guidelines

## Selection

Prefer original papers from publisher, conference, or author-maintained sources. Include foundational methods and recent applications across the repository's research areas. Match a candidate to a concrete module or evaluation question. Verify title, authors, publication year, venue or preprint status, and DOI or persistent identifier. Check existing notes by identifier and title before adding a paper; journal and preprint versions normally share one note.

## Paper Note

Use `papers/YEAR-firstauthor-short-title.md`. Keep the note concise, normally 150-250 words, and include:

- Citation and a working primary-source link.
- Publication status, review date, and evidence inspected: full text with section references, or explicitly abstract-only.
- What the authors studied and what they reported.
- Relevance to a specific repository component, labeled as our interpretation.
- Limitations and conditions that affect transfer to this project.
- One testable next step, labeled as proposed rather than completed.

Paraphrase in original language. Do not copy abstracts, tables, or paper PDFs into the repository. Report numerical findings only with their dataset, metric, and experimental context. Do not turn simulation findings into deployment claims. Preserve normal author attribution while keeping notes focused on methods and evidence; omit personal and organizational background.

## Publication

Add one paper per commit, update the index and changelog, and refine the synthesis only when the evidence supports it. Check relative links, duplicate identifiers, accuracy, and the distinction between reported findings and proposed applications. Keep corrections visible in a subsequent commit. Publication should add useful evidence, not merely an activity entry.
